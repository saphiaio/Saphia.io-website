Markets · Rare Earths

Rare Earths

Seventeen elements, one refiner. China mines roughly 70% of the world's rare-earth oxides and separates over 85%, and 98–99% of the heavy elements that make a permanent magnet survive heat. Its 2025–26 export-licensing regime is the most consequential commodity policy of the decade. This page tracks price structure, concentration and the ex-China build-out. No live price is carried.

The heavy-element dependency has a second layer: an estimated 70% of China's own dysprosium and terbium feedstock is ionic-clay ore from Kachin State, Myanmar, so disruption there transmits directly into global heavy prices.7 Med Reserves are less concentrated than output, China holds 44 Mt of an estimated global base that also includes Brazil, India, Australia and Vietnam.1

The $110/kg Department of Defense floor for MP Materials (with profit-sharing above it) was set at roughly double 2025 market levels; it now functions as both floor and soft ceiling for Western buyers, who will not pay materially more when contracted supply is available near it.3 6 In Q1 2026 nearly half of MP's revenue reportedly came from the price-protection agreement rather than market sales.8 Low

Rough-diamond parallel: a producer-set price with an offtake guarantee is a sight system with a sovereign as the sightholder. It stabilises the producer and delays price discovery. The natural diamonds page documented what happens when the guaranteed buyer's willingness to hold inventory ends; here the buyer is a defence ministry with no balance-sheet constraint, which makes the floor more durable and the market signal less informative. Aggregate allied separated NdPr capacity is estimated at 3,500–4,500 tpa against US defence and semiconductor demand alone of 5,000–10,000 tpa REO-equivalent.8 Low Bloomberg Intelligence projects China's NdPr share falling from c.90% (2024) to 69% by 2030, while a global NdPr shortfall of up to 36% by 2030 remains possible.12 Med Western cost disadvantage is cited at 2–4× ($40–60/kg vs $15–25/kg in China), with 5–7-year facility timelines.6 Low

Glut risk is real. Several analysts expect a regional surplus of light oxides and magnets by 2027–28 as Vulcan, Neo and others commission, exposing new plants to Chinese price cuts unless floors and offtakes are extended.5 The cure for high prices is high prices, the 2011–12 rare-earth bubble ended exactly this way. Med

VGulf angle

Capital and free-zone infrastructure, not geology.

The Gulf has negligible rare-earth geology but three relevant assets: sovereign capital able to fund ex-China separation plants at a cost of capital Western juniors cannot match; free-zone trading and vaulting infrastructure already used for gold and diamonds; and neutral-jurisdiction positioning between the US price-floor bloc and Chinese supply. DMCC's Future of Trade 2026 critical-minerals brief explicitly frames refining concentration and export controls as a trade-hub opportunity.13 Med

The plausible Gulf role is the one it plays in gold: a warehousing, tendering and financing centre for separated oxides and magnet alloys, rather than a producer. The tender model from rough diamonds, sealed bids on graded parcels, is directly transferable to a material that currently trades on opaque bilateral contracts and a Shanghai benchmark. Hyp
