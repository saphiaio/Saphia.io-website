Mānuka Honey

A monofloral honey from one shrub, sold at ten times the price of ordinary honey on the strength of a methylglyoxal number and a Māori name, which New Zealand spent eight years and lost trying to trademark. This page covers the grading systems, the export economics after a pandemic-era boom and bust, the hive collapse behind the supply side, the trans-Tasman naming war, and the counterfeit problem that a science definition was meant to solve. Three layers: a legal definition, a trademarked grade, and competing commercial scales.

The MPI legal test and UMFHA grading check different markers, which is why both can be true of one jar and neither substitutes for the other. The sector is fragmented, over 1,600 businesses, multiple grading systems creating consumer confusion, voluntary bodies without enforcement power, and failed attempts to unify under one organisation.4 1 High Mānuka is classified as a "standard food" in New Zealand and in the EU, US and China, which limits the health claims that can be made for it.1

Diamond parallel: MGO is the carat weight, one measurable number that the whole retail price ladder hangs on, and UMF is the laboratory report. The difference is that the "GIA" here is a producers' association that only grades its own members, and the state definition it sits on top of is a customs test, not a quality grade. Hyp

Apiculture New Zealand's reading of the cycle: a bumper 2020 crop coincided with importers stockpiling against supply-chain uncertainty; when they stopped, demand fell back and the industry was left holding up to three years of sales in storage.7 Export value has fallen 13% from the 2020 peak while unit prices slid, and bulk prices have kept declining even as hive numbers dropped 42%, the classic signature of a stockpile being worked off rather than of a supply shortage.1 High

IIIThe supply side

A wild shrub, a short flowering, and a hive count that halved.

Leptospermum scoparium flowers for a few weeks in late spring and early summer; hives are trucked into remote scrub, often on Māori-owned land, and the honey's MGO develops from dihydroxyacetone in the nectar as it matures. The economics therefore depend on land access, weather in a narrow window, and the number of hives an operator can place, and hive numbers have fallen 42% since 2019 as small beekeepers left the industry.1 Med

IVThe naming war

Eight years, a lost case, and a precedent for every geographic name.

DateEventOutcome
2015 onwardNew Zealand producers seek to trademark "Manuka Honey" in New Zealand, the UK, EU, US and ChinaOpposed by the Australian Manuka Honey Association with support from a UK retail chain2 8
2020Mānuka Charitable Trust established; files replacement applicationsContinues the appeals; disputes a NZ$1.7 m government loan obligation2
2023Intellectual Property Office of New Zealand rules against the application, "Manuka" descriptive, not distinctiveNot appealed; abandoned; costs order made3 8
ResultAustralian producers may sell "manuka" honey from the same plant; UMF™ remains a New Zealand-only trademarkThe premium now rests on UMF and the MPI definition, not on the word3 4

The Australian position was that a New Zealand monopoly on a plant name would set a controversial international precedent; the New Zealand position was that the word is Māori and the product's authenticity is bound to its origin. Both are genuine industry positions rather than verdicts on quality, and the trademark question is separate from the MPI export definition.2 3 High

Compare Champagne, Port and Kobe: each protected a name through a geographical indication regime rather than a trademark, and each succeeded because the name was tied to a place rather than to a species that grows across a border. Mānuka's defeat is the counter-example the diamond trade should note when it argues about "Botswana" or "Canadamark" as origin brands. Hyp

The industry's own view is that export regulations and the UMF mark "provide a level of guarantee to an overseas consumer", a guarantee that stops at the New Zealand border, since neither the MPI test nor UMFHA has jurisdiction over honey blended or relabelled in the destination market.2 Med
