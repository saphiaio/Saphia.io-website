Why Σ3

Price discovery exists but is thin: a deep monthly auction record for standard age statements, and a handful of lots a year for the trophies, several of which failed to sell in 2024 before the 2026 record. The category was defined only in 2021 and enforced from 2024, so a bottle labelled before then may contain imported spirit, and the market now prices that distinction. Supply is controlled by two companies that allocate by ballot in Japan and ration export, which is both the source of the premium and the reason a holder cannot rely on it. Bottles are easy to store and insure, and the entry price is low; the exit for anything above a Yamazaki 18 is a consignment to a specialist and a wait for the right sale.

IIWhat "Japanese whisky" means

Until 2021 it meant nothing in law; the market now prices the label date.

For most of the category's history, whisky imported in bulk from Scotland or Canada could be blended, bottled and sold as Japanese. The Japan Spirits & Liqueurs Makers Association enacted a voluntary labelling standard on 1 April 2021 with a three-year transition: to be labelled Japanese Whisky, the water must be Japanese, saccharification, fermentation and distillation must take place at a distillery in Japan, distillation below 95% ABV, maturation in wood in Japan for at least three years, and bottling in Japan. Suntory confirmed all exports compliant. In March 2025 the JSLMA announced it would apply for geographical-indication status, which would make the standard enforceable against non-members.6 7 High

IIISupply and allocation

Two companies decide what is scarce.

Suntory (Yamazaki, Hakushu, Chita; Hibiki blend) and Nikka (Yoichi, Miyagikyo; Taketsuru blend) distil the overwhelming majority of aged Japanese whisky. Both ran down stocks in the 1990s–2000s lull, both withdrew age statements in the 2010s when demand returned, and both now allocate domestic releases by lottery and ration export. The scarcity is therefore managed, and the premium a holder enjoys is at the discretion of the producer's release calendar. Exports slowed in 2023 for the first time, which the trade read as the first question over whether the category had peaked.5 Med

Karuizawa and Hanyu are the Port Ellen and Brora of Japan: closed in 2000, their remaining casks bought by independent bottlers and released in small batches whose supply can only fall. They hold the records for the same reason Old Masters do, a fixed stock that museums (here, collectors in Taiwan and Hong Kong) remove from circulation. The two Karuizawa 1999 casks sold in London in March 2026 may be the last of that liquid in wood. Hyp4

Below the two majors, a new generation of distilleries (Chichibu since 2008, Shizuoka, Akkeshi, Kanosuke, Mars) is producing compliant single malt whose oldest stock is now in its teens. Chichibu bottlings already command auction premiums; the others are a bet on the category rather than on a stock.8 Med
