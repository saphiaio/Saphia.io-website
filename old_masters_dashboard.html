Why Σ3

Every work is unique and most have not traded in a generation, so value rests on attribution, condition and a thin set of comparables. Exit runs through a few major auction sales a year or a dealer's private placement that may take months to arrange, with guarantees now common at the top. A change of attribution can move a picture's value by an order of magnitude or more in either direction, and export licences can block the sale altogether.

The smallest of the fine-art auction sectors by value and the only one whose supply is permanently fixed: nothing more will be painted, and every year museums remove works from circulation for good. The 2024 trough was a fifteen-year low; 2025 rose 30% and the first half of 2026 produced record weeks in both New York and London. This page covers the sector's size, the record book, who buys and why, how attribution makes and unmakes value, and what to watch. No prices are carried as live values.

ISize and cycle

A fixed stock, a shrinking free float, and a price series that moves on a handful of lots.

Arts Economics defines the sector as artists born between 1250 and 1821, with "European Old Masters" a subset. In 2025 public auction sales in the wider sector reached just under $1.2 bn, up 30%, after a 2024 total that was the lowest in fifteen years. European Old Masters rose 38% to $535 m while the number of lots sold fell 6%: the recovery was in price per lot, not volume.1 2 High

The rotation is the story of the report: contemporary auction sales fell for a fourth consecutive year while Impressionist (+47%) and Old Masters (+30%) recovered. Both heads of department in London describe buyers crossing over from contemporary art and finding Old Masters "comparatively good value"; 23% of lots sold in Sotheby's July evening sale went to collectors new to the category.2 7 High

Supply is the inverse of every other market on this site. The stock was fixed by 1800; each museum acquisition, bequest or export bar removes a work permanently; and the Antonello da Messina panel withdrawn from Sotheby's February sale and bought by Italy's Ministry of Culture for $14.9 m is a live example. The free float shrinks every year while the money chasing it does not. Hyp5

The July results show the split. Christie's spread value across 39 lots with nothing above £10 m and sold 90%; Sotheby's carried the two headline prices and failed to sell ten lots, including twelve pictures with ambitious estimates that found no bidder. ARTnews' reading is a market "increasingly unforgiving for more ordinary material".6 7 High

Guarantees and single bidders. The Canaletto was guaranteed and backed by an irrevocable bid; eleven of Christie's 37 sold February lots were guaranteed; the Lawrence sold to one bidder. A headline price that is pre-sold or uncontested is a floor set by the house and the consignor, not a market clearing price.3 7 High

IIIAttribution and provenance

In few markets does a change of label move price so far in both directions — by an order of magnitude or more in the clearest cases.

Three of 2026's headline lots were attribution events. The Michelangelo foot was recently attributed; the £8 m Rembrandt had been catalogued as an anonymous Dutch painter; the Sotheby's London sale also carried a Botticelli "and Associate", the house's own hedge on a £2–3 m estimate. Downgrades run the other way and rarely make the press: a picture reassigned from master to workshop or follower loses most of its value overnight and may be unsaleable at the top houses.4 6 High

Title is the second risk. Old Master pictures have long chains of ownership through war, revolution and confiscation; restitution claims, Nazi-era provenance gaps and national export controls (Italy's pre-emption of the Antonello, the UK's deferral system) can delay, block or reprice a sale after the hammer. Several lots in February's New York sales were marketed as newly restituted, which is both a supply source and a reminder of the claims that remain.4 5 High

Diamond parallel: an Old Master is a stone with no laboratory. The certificate is a scholar's opinion, published in a catalogue raisonné or given privately, and it can be withdrawn. The nearest equivalent to a GIA report is a technical examination, infrared reflectography, dendrochronology, pigment analysis, which can rule an attribution out but seldom rules one in. Hyp

Demand has broadened by geography and by age. The Laocoön drew bidders from Asia and Europe; Sotheby's reports a record 4.9 bidders per lot across all categories in the first half of 2026 and the move to the Breuer doubling visitor traffic. Whether crossover contemporary buyers stay once the "good value" argument is exhausted is the open question for 2027.6 8 Med
