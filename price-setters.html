Who sets the price

Every market page carries a Supply concentration line: where along the chain the power to set price sits. This page reads the 51 lines together. In some markets the price is made by trades anyone can see; in others it is set by one seller, one board or one room the holder is not in. The difference decides what a quoted price is worth.

IDiscovered or administered

A price is discovered when it is the result of trades between many buyers and sellers who can each walk away, and administered when one party decides it and the others decide only whether to deal. Most of the 51 markets sit between the two: a price list that is really a seller's asking price, an auction where one hammer sets the comparable for a decade, a benchmark that is set in a room open to members only.

The Supply concentration line on each page records where the power sits. It is a structural rating, not a judgement of conduct: the Québec Federation administers the maple price under provincial law and publishes it; De Beers sells about a third of the world's rough to a fixed list of buyers at prices it sets 1 20; Hermès allocates Birkins by client and the resale premium is the gap between its price and the market's. 21 None of this is hidden. What it means for a holder is that the quoted price is a decision, and decisions change.

The register in Section III sorts the markets by regime. The pattern that emerges is that the power to set price rarely sits at the mine, the vineyard or the farm. It sits one or two steps up the chain, at the refiner, the separator, the marketing board, the house or the saleroom, and the sector pages' concentration lines say so: gold is "Medium, refining (mine Low)"; copper "High, refining (mine Medium)"; rare earths "High, separation (mining lower)". 20 22 23

VRooms the holder is not in

In many markets the price is discovered honestly, but among a closed set of participants. The private holder sees the result and pays an intermediary to reach it.

Members onlyTrade platforms

Liv-ex trades only between professionals; a private holder sells through a merchant who takes 10% or more. Greysheet bids are dealer-to-dealer. The Parma Borsa Merci lists prices for trade buyers. The price is real; the access is not. 36

Licensed buyersPrimary auctions

Toyosu, the Japanese carcass auctions, Gemfields' rough tenders, the Kimberley-registered diamond tenders, Paspaley's pearl sales and the prefectural tencha auctions are open to licensed trade buyers. The holder of a fish, a stone or a leaf does not bid; the holder of equity in the buyer does, by proxy.

The chosen listSights and allocations

De Beers chooses its sightholders and what goes in their boxes; Hermès chooses who may buy a Birkin; Suntory allocates by ballot; the gallery chooses the collector. A secondary market forms immediately outside the door, and its premium or discount to the administered price is the only free price in the market. 26 21

The list is not the priceAsking schedules

The Rapaport list is sellers' asking prices and "isn't a record of actual transaction prices"; the sector page puts trade prices 20–40% below it and a private seller's realisation at 40–60% of retail. Dealer price ladders for oud, rare earths and saffron work the same way: the quote is the dealer's, in both directions. 24 37

VIThree patterns in the record

Pattern 1Chain

Power sits one step above the source

Mines, vineyards, farms and finders are dispersed in almost every market here. Concentration appears at the refiner, the separator, the marketing board, the house or the saleroom. The sector pages that split the line ("refining (mine Low)") are the ones to read for where a holder's price is actually made.

Watch: Chinese separation share in rare earths; refinery consolidation in copper and gold

Pattern 2Two tiers

An administered price breeds a free one next to it

Wherever one party sets the price and chooses the buyer, a second market forms outside the door: secondary sight boxes, the Birkin resale premium, the Japanese whisky ballot premium, the maple reserve stock, art flipped from gallery to auction. The gap between the two prices is the measure of how far the administered price is from what the market would pay.

Watch: the secondary box premium to De Beers' sight price; the Birkin resale ratio; ballot-to-auction gaps

Pattern 3Grip

When the administrator loses control, the fall is fastest

De Beers held roughly 85% of rough and now holds a third; its parent wrote down $6.8bn in three years and lab-grown took the engagement ring. The cask firms that quoted their own prices were insolvent by 2025. An administered price is stable until it is not, and the sector pages for diamonds and casks record what the transition cost holders. 3 38

Watch: Botswana's stake in De Beers and the sight system's future; maple reserve drawdowns

Derived observation

The Liquidity line on the sector pages is highest where the price is discovered on an exchange or benchmark, and lowest where it is administered or set by dealer quote. A holder cannot change who sets the price, but can read the regime before buying and know, for a cask or a polished stone, that the number quoted at entry is the seller's and the number at exit will be the buyer's. VIIIndicators to monitor

  • Any change of ownership at De Beers and whether the sight system survives it; Botswana holds 15% with pre-emptive rights Med 3.
  • The Québec Federation's reserve level and any revision of the administered bulk price after a short season.
  • Publication of any transaction-based diamond price index, which would move polished diamonds from Asking list to Trade platform.
  • Hermès' and the watch brands' allocation policies, and the resale premium that measures them.
  • Whether Liv-ex or any trade platform opens to private holders.
  • Chinese export controls on rare-earth separation and the ex-China dealer premium.
