A 150–330 kt deficit is only 0.5–1.1% of a c.28.7 Mt refined market, but with inelastic demand the marginal tonne sets the price for the whole market, which is why the metal set an all-time high above $6/lb on COMEX in January 2026 while Chinese demand was described as muted.5 10 Med Chinese smelters, short of concentrate, cut production; one analysis puts the refined deficit above 350 kt if a 20% Chinese cut persists through Q4 2026.3 Low

Why a market of 23 Mt turned on a handful of mines.

The structural point is concentration: the twenty largest mines supply 36% of the world, most are ageing, and no new mine replaces them; Benchmark's phrase is "the sector's dependence on a small group of major mines".1 10 High

China refined six times the DRC's volume in 2025 and, in November 2025, its refined-copper exports rose c.1,100% year on year, a country shifting from importer to exporter of cathode as domestic demand softened.10 12 13 Med The refining bottleneck, not the mine, is the binding constraint in 2026: smelters lack concentrate even where mine output rises.4

VPolicy: tariffs and critical-mineral status

The United States taxes its own supply chain.

MeasureDetailConfidence
Section 232 copper tariff, 50%Proclamation "Adjusting Imports of Copper into the United States", effective 1 Aug 2025, on cathode, refined copper, alloys and defined downstream products; stacks on the 10% Section 122 baseline and any Section 301 surcharge; FTAs with Chile, Peru and USMCA partners do not exempt it beyond narrow carve-outs; still in force May 202612 15Med
US import sourcesRefined copper imports 2024: Chile 65%, Canada 17%, Mexico 9%, Peru 6% (USGS); Chile c.$4.5 bn of cathode from Codelco, Anglo American Sur, Escondida, Antofagasta16 15High
US net import reliance45% (2024) to an estimated 57% (2025) as domestic mine output fell 9–10% and refined imports hit a record c.1.63–1.70 Mt on tariff front-loading; USGS critical-mineral designation13Med
Chinese export flowsRefined exports +1,100% year on year in Nov 202512Med

The tariff protects a refining base that is shrinking: domestic refined output fell an estimated 9–10% in 2025 on maintenance and grades, and recycled copper covers roughly 30–35% of US supply.13 The policy raises the landed cost of cathode for US fabricators without adding a tonne of domestic supply in the forecast horizon.
