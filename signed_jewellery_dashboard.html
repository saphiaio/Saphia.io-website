Why Σ3

A signed piece has a maker, a date and often an archive number, which gives it something a loose stone lacks: an identity that can be verified. But the premium that identity carries, frequently several times the value of the materials, is set by sentiment and fashion. Valuation is by specialist against recent auction comparables; exit is two Geneva seasons a year or a dealer; and the risks are forged signatures, replaced stones and pieces the house will not confirm.

Jewellery is the one item every estate contains, and the one where the gap between what it is worth as materials and what it is worth as an object is widest. A Cartier Art Deco bracelet and an anonymous one of the same stones and date differ in price by a multiple, not a margin. This page covers what the signature means, the size and current cycle of the market, the record book, how pieces are authenticated and why the house archive matters, and what to watch. IWhat "signed" means

A maker's mark, a serial number and, ideally, a line in the archive.

A signed jewel carries the house name and usually a serial or inventory number engraved on the mount, with French assay and maker's marks where the piece was made in Paris. The number is the key: Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels and Boucheron hold archives that can confirm a piece's date, original design and first client, and issue a certificate for a fee. Pieces by houses without a working archive, or whose records were lost, trade on the signature and the expert's eye alone.

IISize and the 2025–26 cycle

Reported inside "luxury"; the houses do not publish a signed-jewellery series.

Neither house separates signed jewellery from jewellery, or jewellery from luxury, in its public figures. What is reported: Sotheby's luxury turnover rose 22% in 2025 to $2.7 bn and Christie's 17% to $795 m; Sotheby's jewellery alone made $270 m in 2024; and both houses' Geneva sales in May 2026 cleared almost everything offered. The signed premium is visible in the lot results rather than the totals: Cartier's tiara at nearly three times low estimate, a Chaumet Kashmir sapphire at $3.5 m, a Boucheron Art Deco necklace, all in one sale.126 High for the house figures; Low for any estimate of the signed segment's size.

IVAuthentication and the archive

The signature is the easiest part to fake and the archive is the only check.

No archiveHouses whose records are lost or who decline requestsExpert opinion only; priced accordingly
TitleStolen jewellery is portable and anonymous once stones are unsetInsurance registers; auction houses check the Art Loss Register; private sales do not
Parallel to watches: the serial number and the manufacturer's archive do the same work as a watch's extract from the archives. The difference is that a watch is rarely altered, while jewellery has been remounted, shortened and restoned for a century, and a confirmed, unaltered period piece is correspondingly scarcer. Hyp
