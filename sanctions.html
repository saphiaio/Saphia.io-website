Sanctions, tariffs and origin

In a scarce-asset market the state rarely sets the price, but it often decides where a thing may go and what it may be called when it gets there. This page reads the sanctions, tariffs, export controls and species listings recorded on the market pages together: which instrument applies, what it does to the route, and how an origin rule reshapes where a market trades.

IFour instruments

Four kinds of state measure appear across the market pages, and they do different things to a holder. A sanction bars an origin from a market altogether: Russian diamonds from the G7, Myanmar jade from sanctioning jurisdictions, Iranian saffron from the banking system, Cuban cigars from the United States. A tariff taxes an origin at the border and moves the price gap between origins: US duties on polished diamonds by country of cutting, on Brazilian coffee, Tunisian olive oil, Japanese tea and, for fifteen months, Scotch. An export control is applied by the producing state: Chinese licences on seven rare-earth elements, Italy's pre-emption of an Old Master, Somaliland's licensing of resin, Saudi limits on farmland. A species listing under CITES conditions every cross-border move of a living or once-living thing: sturgeon, falcons, agarwood, Boswellia since 2025, Brazilian rosewood on a pre-1969 guitar.

What they share is that they act on origin, and origin is the claim the Provenance page found hardest to prove. A sanction on Russian rough is only as good as the ability to tell Russian rough from Botswanan after both have passed through Surat; a tariff by country of cutting requires a customs officer to know where a stone was cut. Every instrument on this page therefore creates a demand for the same thing: a document, a ledger or a laboratory that can say where a thing came from. The G7 diamond regime is the clearest case, and Section III takes it in detail.

The second thing they share is that they move trade rather than stop it. Saffron passes through Dubai and La Mancha labels; jade has "no route at all" into sanctioning jurisdictions and a full one in Hong Kong; rare earths outside China trade at a dealer premium that is the price of the licence. Section V follows where the trade goes.

IIICase: Russian diamonds

The one sanction in the register built on a traceability requirement rather than a border check, and so the test of whether origin can be enforced in a market that mixes its goods.

DateMeasureConfidenceSource
11 Mar 2022US bars import of non-industrial diamonds mined in RussiaHigh4

1 Mar 2024Ban extended to Russian diamonds processed in third countries, at 1.0 ct and above, closing the "substantial transformation" route through IndiaHigh2 4
1 Sep 2024Threshold lowered to 0.5 ct; extended to lab-grown diamonds and to jewellery and watches incorporating diamonds; traceability evidence requiredHigh3 5
2024–Antwerp designated the verification node; a G7 certificate issued for imported natural rough of 0.5 ct and above of non-Russian origin; none for polishedMed3 6
May 2026GIA agrees a 30% stake in De Beers' Tracr provenance ledger, which has registered more than 5m rough stonesHigh45
What it required

A statement of origin for every stone above half a carat crossing a G7 border, when about 90% of the world's polished is cut in India from mixed parcels. 4 20 The Kimberley Process certifies that rough is not conflict-financed, not which mine it came from; the sanction needed a second document the trade did not have.

What it built

A verification hub in Antwerp, a certificate for compliant rough, and a commercial reason for the industry's provenance ledgers, which the Tokenisation page records had been attempted for a decade without a business case. The sanction supplied one.

What it moved

Alrosa's goods to buyers outside the G7, Indian cutting into a documented chain, and the question of origin from a marketing claim to a customs requirement. The sector page's Provenance risk line for rough reads "KP certificate, G7 sanctions on Russian origin": the sanction is now part of the provenance. 46

Derived observation

For the exchange-traded metals the tariff never had to be imposed to move the market: the threat alone pulled copper and platinum into US warehouses and opened a location premium that then collapsed when the decision lapsed. For the collectibles and consumables the tariff was imposed and either lifted (Scotch, coffee) or absorbed into the price list (watches). In neither case did it change what the asset was; it changed where it was worth holding it. Hyp

VWhere the trade moves to

An origin rule in one jurisdiction creates a hub in another. The sector pages name the hubs.

DubaiSaffron, rough diamonds, rare earths

Iranian saffron passes through Dubai to escape banking sanctions; rough diamonds tender in Dubai alongside Antwerp; the rare-earths page notes free-zone trading and vaulting already used for gold and diamonds. 23 46 26

Hong KongJade, whisky, gemstones

The only auction depth for jadeite and the route for Myanmar material that the West will not take; record Japanese whisky sales "for a reason"; one of three cities for the top coloured-stone sales. 21 47

La ManchaSaffron

Spanish labels on Iranian threads: re-exporters capture the origin premium the grower never sees, and customs values for Spanish saffron run above Afghan. 23

AntwerpDiamonds

Designated by the EU as the verification node for the G7 regime, which the Gulf trade press read as a bid to centralise authority over the sector. The sanction made a hub as much as it closed one. Med 3 7

VIThree patterns in the record

Pattern 1Document

Every origin rule creates a paper requirement

G7 certificates, CITES permits, Kimberley certificates, pre-convention certificates for rosewood, export licences for an Old Master: the measure is enforced by a document, and the document becomes part of the asset. A falcon without its papers, a guitar without its certificate and a stone without its origin record are each worth less than the same object with them.

Watch: whether the diamond regime's traceability requirement is extended to polished and to lower thresholds

Pattern 2Hub

Trade moves to the nearest jurisdiction that does not ask

Dubai for saffron and rough, Hong Kong for jade and whisky, Spanish labels for Iranian threads. The sanctioned origin does not leave the market; it leaves the sanctioning jurisdiction, and the holder in that jurisdiction loses a route rather than the asset losing a buyer.

Watch: Hong Kong and Dubai auction calendars for categories the West restricts

Pattern 3Location

A tariff prices where a thing is, not what it is

Copper and platinum moved into US warehouses on a threat and were stranded when it lapsed; Scotch paid 10% for fifteen months and then did not; a polished stone's cutting country now carries a duty rate. None of these changed the asset. They changed the value of holding it on one side of a border rather than the other, which is a cost the sector pages' Liquidity lines do not capture and this page does.

Watch: COMEX–LME and US–London premiums as the live price of a tariff threat

VIIIndicators to monitor

  • Any lowering of the G7 diamond threshold below 0.5 ct, or extension of G7 certification to polished stones, which the EU FAQ leaves open 5 6.
  • Chinese rare-earth export licensing after the current pause and the US defence ban from 1 Jan 2027 26.
  • The US refined-copper tariff decision, lapsed in September 2026 without announcement 27.
  • Enforcement of the 2025 CITES listing of Boswellia, and whether it reaches resin already in trade 41.
  • The US ban on Russian enriched uranium from 2028 and the waivers issued before it 25.
  • Any change in US or EU sanctions on Myanmar's gems enterprise, which would reopen or close the Western route for jade.
