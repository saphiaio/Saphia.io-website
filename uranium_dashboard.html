Why Σ2

An individual cannot hold uranium: it is licensed material, stored only at converters' facilities in Canada, France and the United States. But a listed closed-end trust holds 82 million pounds there and prices it daily, so exposure is a brokerage trade with full transparency on what is owned. The grade stops at Σ2 because the trust's unit can trade 10–15% away from the metal it holds, because the published spot price is a consultants' estimate from a thin market, and because the material is subject to export control and, for Russian-origin supply, sanction.

The only energy commodity whose buyers are a few hundred reactor operators, whose sellers are two companies and a dozen smaller ones, and whose price is not set on any exchange. Uranium does not trade openly; utilities and producers sign private contracts, two consultancies infer a price from what they are told, and a Toronto trust that buys physical pounds has since 2021 been the marginal buyer in the spot market. This page covers the two-price system, supply concentration, the demand case and the contracting cycle, the routes to exposure and what each actually holds, and what to watch. ITwo prices, one of them real

The headline is the spot price; reactors are fuelled on the term price.

Uranium has no exchange. UxC and TradeTech publish month-end spot and long-term indicators derived from the transactions reported to them; Cameco republishes the industry average. Spot covers the discretionary, near-term slice of the market, around 55 m lb a year and falling; long-term contracts, 116 m lb in 2025 and rising, are how utilities actually buy, at prices with floors and ceilings that escalate over years. In 2026 the term indicator rose every month to $97/lb while spot posted three down months and settled near $90.134 High

The spot market is thin enough that one buyer moves it. Sprott's 2.3 m lb purchase at the end of September 2025 took spot to a fifteen-month high; its 8.67 m lb of buying in 2025 was nearly three times 2024. When the trust trades at a discount it cannot issue units and stops buying, and spot drifts. The headline price is partly a measure of fund flows. High56

New mines take seven to ten years from decision to first production, and the price collapse of 2011–20 stopped most development. Supply responds, but on a timetable longer than any contract cycle, which is why the term price has risen without pause while spot stalls. Med

IIIDemand and the contracting cycle

Reactors are inelastic buyers; the question is when they sign.

Nuclear fuel is a small share of a reactor's cost, so utilities do not cut consumption when the price rises; they delay signing. The World Nuclear Association projects demand up 28% by 2030 on life extensions, restarts (Japan, Palisades, Three Mile Island 1) and new builds in China, India and the Gulf; data-centre power agreements have added a buyer class that did not exist in 2022. Contracting volumes recovered from 2023, and Sprott's 2026 outlook calls the cycle "re-accelerating" on policy clarity.58 Med

Parallel to gold ETFs: a trust holding allocated metal in a vault, priced daily. The difference is the discount. A gold ETF arbitrages to NAV because authorised participants can redeem for bars; SPUT has no redemption mechanism, so a 13% gap between the unit and the uranium can sit open for months.
