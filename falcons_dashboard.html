Why Σ4

The market is real and growing, and it is one market: Gulf falconers, bidding in Riyadh and Abu Dhabi between August and November, for birds that will hunt or race that winter. Price discovery exists for three months a year and is broadcast live, but there is no bid outside the region and none outside the season. The asset is alive, depreciates after its first moult, can die of aspergillosis or a collision, and cannot cross a border without a CITES permit and, in the Gulf, a falcon passport. Title is a ring number and a certificate, which is adequate; custody means a falconer, a mews and a hospital. A buyer is acquiring a season of sport, and the record prices are for the rarest colour of the rarest hybrid, which the breeder can make again next year.

Falconry is on UNESCO's intangible-heritage list and the Gulf states have built a modern market around it: licensed breeders, televised auctions, falcon hospitals, racing with seven-figure prize funds and a conservation apparatus for the wild populations. This page covers the auction record, what a buyer is actually paying for, the regulatory structure that makes the bird a tradeable asset, the ways it loses value, and what to watch. The series is short and steep: the top lot tripled from 2024 to 2025 and the auction's volume grew by a quarter, with sessions broadcast live on television and streamed. The all-time record, SR1.75 m in 2021, predates the current growth; the 2026 top lot is the second-highest ever. The buyer's own account of the SR1.3 m bird, "rare birds are priceless", is the market's valuation method stated plainly.1 3 5 High

Two markets again. Captive-bred birds from licensed farms are the auction; wild-taken birds, legally trapped under quota in Mongolia, Kazakhstan or Pakistan and prized by traditional falconers for hunting ability, are a separate and less transparent trade that the exhibition record has now brought into the open. Wild-taken sakers and peregrines are the conservation concern; the breeders' auction is partly the answer to it. Med2

The bird is sold in August for a season that runs to March. Its value to the buyer is sporting and social: a hunting trip, a race entry, a place in a majlis. The breeder's value is the pair that produced it, which is why the supply side looks like breeding stallions and the demand side like a season ticket. Hyp

IIIPermits, passports and the farms

Why a falcon can be sold at all.

All falcons are listed under CITES: peregrine and gyrfalcon on Appendix I, saker on Appendix II. A captive-bred bird from a registered breeder may be traded internationally with a CITES certificate of captive breeding and a closed leg ring fitted as a chick; wild-taken birds may move only under national export quota. The UAE introduced a falcon passport in 2002, since adopted across the GCC, which records the bird, its ring and microchip and its owner and permits travel with the falconer; Saudi Arabia's National Center for Falcons and the Saudi Falcons Club (2017) regulate the domestic market, the auctions and the breeding-farm licences.3 6 Med

Custody is the working life of a falconer: daily weighing, feeding, flying, a mews, transport in the heat, and veterinary care that the Gulf has built world-class hospitals to provide. It is the second-heaviest custody burden on this site after racehorses, with the difference that a horse at least has a residual value as breeding stock; a falcon's residual is the next season. Med
