IPrice

A parabolic January 2026, then the sharpest retracement of any major metal.

Silver set its dollar record of $121.67 on 29 January 2026, the same session in which gold peaked.3 The subsequent retracement was steeper than gold's, consistent with silver's higher beta to rates and growth and its lack of an official-sector buyer to absorb selling.2 High

The gold:silver ratio is the cleanest relative-value gauge: it reached a 100:1 extreme in April 2025 and had compressed to c.61:1 by April 2026 as silver outperformed into and out of its peak.9 A ratio above 80 has historically coincided with investment demand leaving the metal; below 60 with speculative excess. Q3 2026, recorded October. Silver traded around $61 in early October, about half its January record, down ~7% on the month and still ~26% above a year earlier.1112 Against gold near $4,140 that puts the gold:silver ratio at roughly 67:1, up from c.61:1 in April: silver has given back more than gold since spring, consistent with the solar demand cut below. Forecast revision as a signal

J.P. Morgan cut its Q4 2026 silver forecast from $90 to $63 during 2026. The driver cited was not the Fed but demand destruction in solar, sized at roughly 60 Moz for the year, larger than the entire projected 2026 deficit.5 Recorded as a house view at the date made; not updated. Med

LONGi, the largest module maker, told investors on 5 January 2026 that it would mass-produce base-metal (copper) cells from Q2, the clearest statement yet that the record price is engineering silver out of its largest industrial use.2 Installations can keep rising while silver demand falls; a headline about record solar deployment says nothing about loading per cell.5

Direct analogue to lab-grown diamonds: substitution triggered by price spikes is sticky. Once a manufacturer has qualified a copper process line, a lower silver price does not bring the volume back. Treat the 2026 solar cut as structural rather than cyclical. Hyp
