Pu-erh Tea

Σ4Restricted
Physical · dry-stored · 20 Oct 2026

A century-old cake sells for HK$3.5 m while the factory tea that the speculative market actually trades has lost four-fifths of its value since 2021; the asset appreciates only if stored correctly for decades and is the most counterfeited tea in the world.

Why Σ4

Two markets share a name. Antique and state-era cakes are valued by a handful of Hong Kong specialists on wrapper, ticket, compression and taste, trade a few dozen lots a year, and are forged at every tier. Modern Dayi factory tea has daily quoted prices on the Donghe platform and a wholesale market at Fangcun, which looks like price discovery until one notices that 95% of it is bought to store, not drink, and that the quotes have halved twice in twenty years. Custody is the asset: a cake stored too dry does not age, too wet is ruined, and the storage history cannot be verified from the outside. Exit for anything but current Dayi is a consignment into a thin Hong Kong sale. Title is simple; everything else is not.

Sotheby's held its first dedicated tea sale in December 2021 with a 1900s Chen Yun Hao estimated at HK$900,000–1.2 m, which marks the point at which the category reached the international houses; Poly and L&H had been running dedicated pu-erh sales for a decade. IIThe factory-tea market

Dayi as a quoted security, and what happens to it.

Menghai Tea Factory (state factory No. 2 until privatised in 2004; brand Dayi, TAETEA) produces the 7542 and 7572 recipes that defined the category and the limited series that the speculative market trades. New releases are allocated to franchise stores at a list price; they change hands at Fangcun in Guangzhou at a multiple of it on the day of release, and the Donghe platform quotes bid and offer by batch like a stock screen. A 2020 7542 reached about 50,000 yuan a jian (42 cakes) and was at 29,000 by early 2024; the Xuan Yuan series went from 70,000 yuan a unit in 2017 to 1.88 m in 2021, 1.08 m in early 2023 and 380,000 in June 2025.2 8 High

2007
After the Yunnan earthquake, Fangcun prices fail to rise; CCTV finds 95% of tea bought to store and hoarding to create false scarcity. Prices halve; bankruptcies across Yunnan, Guangdong, Taiwan and Hong Kong.4
2017–21
Second boom, led by Dayi limited series; famous-village maocha (Lao Banzhang, Bingdao) at record prices; producers package for storage rather than drinking.2 9
2023
A futures-style scam built on pu-erh uncovered at Fangcun; Xuan Yuan down 40% from peak.2 4
2024–25
Dayi quotes fall through 2024; SCMP reads the collapse as the end of corporate gift-giving under the anti-corruption campaign and a weak economy; 7542 "still over 600 yuan a cake".2 8
The Donghe screen is price discovery for a closed loop. Buyers are storers, sellers are storers, and the tea is not drunk; when the marginal storer stops buying, there is no consumer bid beneath. The 2007 and 2023–25 falls were both the loop closing. An outside holder of modern Dayi owns an instrument whose only exit is the same loop. Med2 4

The identification column is the valuation. A Red Mark is a Red Mark because of the colour of one character on a wrapper that can be reproduced; a Hao Ji cake has no wrapper at all and is identified by a paper ticket pressed into the leaf. Taste confirms, but only after the cake is broken, which is why the Hong Kong specialists (Sun Sing Tea's Henry Yeung consulted on Sotheby's first sale) are the market's certificate, and why their opinion is the price.7 Med

IVStorage and forgery

The asset is made by the holder, and faked by everyone else.

RiskMechanismMitigation
StorageRaw pu-erh ages by slow microbial and oxidative change that needs humidity and warmth; Hong Kong "traditional" storage ages fast and can go mouldy; Kunming dry storage barely ages at all; the two are different products at thirty yearsDeclared storage history from a known warehouse; buy stacks that have stayed together; accept that the history is unverifiable
ForgeryDayi is the most counterfeited tea brand in the world; antique wrappers and tickets are reproduced; CNNP sold its name and wrappers to any buyer after privatisationSpecialist authentication; auction-house guarantee; provenance from a named Hong Kong tea house
Market concentrationPrices set by a few hundred storers in Guangdong and Hong Kong; gift-giving demand politically exposedNone; this is the market
ConsumptionEvery tasting removes tea; a broken cake is worth less than a whole oneTrade whole cakes and complete stacks (tong of 7)
Exit outside ChinaWestern auction interest is nascent; buyers are in Hong Kong, Guangzhou, TaipeiConsign in Hong Kong; expect 15–25% in fees

Title passes with the cake and the market has no registry, which makes transfer trivial and provenance impossible: there is no equivalent of a bonded warehouse ledger or an auction-house condition report that survives a change of hands. The nearest analogue on this site is whisky casks, with the difference that a cask at least has a warehousekeeper. Hyp
