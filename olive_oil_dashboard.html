Markets · Olive Oil

Olive Oil

A Mediterranean staple that behaved like a luxury for two years: Spanish drought halved the world's largest crop, producer prices in Jaén trebled, consumption fell, then a record 2024/25 harvest cut prices by a third and the trade sold seventy per cent of the next crop in seven months. This page covers the crop-year balance, the price cycle at origin, the geography of supply, consumption's structural shift away from Europe, and the integrity layer. Global olive oil production has tripled over sixty years, and consumption has nearly doubled since 1990/91, driven by non-IOC and non-EU markets while EU consumption has declined since 2004/05. The 2023/24 price shock is suspected of having caused lasting substitution to other oils in some markets.2 3 7 Med

The Italian premium over Spain, Bari at roughly 1.6–1.7 times Jaén through the cycle, is the market's persistent structural feature: Italy consumes more than it produces, and its brands blend imported Spanish and Tunisian oil under Italian labels. Spanish small-farmer body UPA called the record 2024/25 campaign "disappointing" because some buyers pushed origin prices below what growers considered fair even as exports rose 38%.6 Med

Diamond parallel: the 2023–25 cycle is a pure inventory story. With Spanish stocks 70,000 t below the long-term average at the end of September 2025, and 880,000 t, nearly 70% of the 2025/26 crop, sold within seven months, the trade has no buffer if the 2026/27 flowering fertility decline of c.37% reported by OliveA is borne out. Scarcity pricing returns the moment a single origin fails.6 4 Hyp

Consumption growth has come from non-IOC members and from IOC members outside the EU, Türkiye's table-olive consumption rose from 110 kt to 395 kt and Algeria's from 14 kt to 267.5 kt over the same period, while EU consumption has trended down for two decades.3 The EU still supplies roughly 60% of world production and exports c.787 kt to third countries, so the industry's centre of production and its centre of consumption are diverging.8 High

VGrades and integrity

A product defined by chemistry and taste panels, and one of the most adulterated foods in trade.

Grade (IOC standard)DefinitionMarket role
Extra virginMechanical extraction only; free acidity ≤0.8%; no sensory defects on panelThe reference grade for every producer-price series above
VirginAcidity ≤2%; minor defects permittedMid-tier
LampanteNot fit for consumption unrefinedRefined into "olive oil" blends
Refined / "olive oil" / pomaceChemically or physically refined; solvent-extracted pomace oilVolume and food-service tiers

Grade definitions summarised from the IOC trade standard; thresholds should be read from the standard itself for compliance use. Med

The fraud pattern is structural: the price gap between extra virgin and lower grades, the ease of blending, and the dependence on sensory panels create a persistent incentive to mislabel, particularly in the 2023–24 spike, when the gap widened. No verified 2025–26 enforcement statistics were found for this page; none are asserted, and the topic is carried in the indicators. Med

Diamond parallel: "Italian" olive oil is to Spanish and Tunisian bulk what a "Belgian-cut" stone once was to African rough, the label describes where value was added, not where the product came from. The country-of-origin rules that finally forced blend disclosure on EU labels are the equivalent of mine-of-origin certification. Hyp
