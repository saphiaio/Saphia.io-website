Markets · Cocoa

Cocoa

A crop grown by millions of West African smallholders and priced in New York and London, which quadrupled to a record in December 2024, fell almost 80% into March 2026, then rose 70% in a month on floods. This page covers the balance, the disruption history, the two-country duopoly and its regulated farmgate, the demand destruction inside chocolate, and the policy layer. The market's two regimes since 2023 are both weather: a deficit regime in which a small West African shortfall swings the global balance, and a surplus regime in which demand destruction from the spike lingers even as supply returns. Analysts were divided on the July 2026 rally, material tightening versus over-weighting of short-term weather while ICE inventories were rising.3 Med

IIIThe duopoly and the farmgate

Two countries, two marketing boards, one exchange price.

CountryOutput2025–26 developments
Côte d'Ivoirec.1.8 Mt (ING, 2025/26); 2.0–2.1 Mt (later projection); arrivals >1.7 Mt by mid-May 2026Unsold stocks at ports from November 2025; strategic buyback launched 29 Jan 2026; May 2026 mid-crop rainfall uneven and below average; weaker pod survival4 1 6 2
Ghanac.600–650 kt recent seasons vs 800–900 kt historicallyFarmer price cut nearly 29% as world price fell below the regulated farmgate; c.50,000 t unsold at ports as buyers turned away; heavy rain, black pod; COCOBOD warns of a 2026/27 crop down at least 16%, market range 450–550 kt1 7 6 4

Both states set an annual farmgate price in advance and sell forward. When the exchange price collapsed in 2025–26 the regulated farmgate sat above the world price, traders stopped buying, and beans accumulated at Abidjan, San Pedro and Tema, a pricing mismatch that turned a surplus into a procurement crisis for the two governments.1 6 In the boom the reverse held: farmgate prices rose only c.20% in 2024/25 while the world price quadrupled, and the margin accrued to traders and boards, not growers.7 High

Diamond parallel: a fixed producer price set by a state marketing board against a volatile exchange is the Cocobod version of the benefício in Port or the sight price at De Beers, it protects the grower on the way down only until the board runs out of buyers, and it never passes the upside through. Latin American origins, which sell at the exchange price, gained share in the 2026 surplus.5 Hyp

IVDemand destruction

The spike did to chocolate what it did to jewellery.

Manufacturers reformulated recipes, shrank portions and raised prices; the behavioural shifts have proven stickier than expected, which is why the surplus arrived into a smaller market.6 ICE cocoa inventories in US ports fell to an 8.5-month low of 1.7 m bags in November 2025 and then rose to multi-year highs in 2026 as unsold beans were delivered against the exchange.8 6 Chocolate and confectionery remain the dominant end-use, followed by bakery, beverages, dairy, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals.1 High
