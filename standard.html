The ΣΟΦΙΑ Standard

One analytical standard for markets that lack one. This page is the citable statement of the method as applied to every brief: what is measured, on what scale, at what thresholds, and how the Σ grade is formed. It changes only by version, and every version is logged.

Supersedes v1.1 (2 Nov 2026) and v1.0 (Oct 2026)Change log

1. Scope

The Standard applies to physical scarce assets in six families: Gems & Jewellery; Metals, Energy & Strategic Materials; Cellar & Collectibles; Aromatics; Crops; Rare Foods. 50 markets are rated at this version. A market is rated on the form a holder can actually own, named on the brief (for example "physical metal", "a case in bond", "a share in a stallion"). Where the good cannot be held, the brief says so and rates the most direct route to exposure.

2. The sequence

Every market is read in the same seven steps, in the same order: supply, demand, concentration, liquidity, scarcity, provenance, price formation. Steps 3 to 6 produce the four structural scales below. Steps 1, 2 and 7 are described in the brief and are not scored, because they cannot be put on one scale across unrelated markets.

3. Four structural scales

Each is scored 1 to 5 and always shown with its band word: 1–2 Low, 3 Medium, 4–5 High. Scores read in the brief's natural direction and are never combined into a composite: high concentration is a risk to a buyer, high liquidity a comfort to a seller. Thresholds are published so a score can be checked against the evidence on the brief.

ScaleScores 5 (High) whenScores 1 (Low) when
ConcentrationThe top three controllers of the price-setting stage hold three-quarters or more of it, or one holds half. Rated at the stage of the chain where the power to set price sits, named on the brief.Dispersed; the top three hold under half.
LiquidityAn exchange or daily reference price exists and the standard unit clears daily.Private, irregular or ceremonial sales; no reference price.
ScarcitySupply is fixed or falling and cannot expand within five years. Rated on the apex tier where one exists.Supply responds within a year or two, or a substitute exists at scale.
Provenance riskMislabelling above a fifth in published studies, or no independent check exists.Mandatory, independent, forensic control at the point of trade.

Score 3 (Medium) sits between: concentration where the top three hold half to three-quarters; liquidity where discovery is by auction, index or dealer within weeks; scarcity where supply responds with a three- to five-year lag; provenance where certification exists but is voluntary or cannot detect substitution. Scores 2 and 4 are used where the evidence sits clearly between bands.

4. The Σ grade

ΣΟΦΙΑ's opinion of how practical the asset is to hold and to exit. Not a view on return, value or merit. Σ5 is the most practical, Σ1 the least. It is a judgement across six factors, each scored 1 to 5 (5 = least burden), and is not their average: one factor at 1 can hold a grade down on its own, and the brief says which.

5. Holding-level rules

A holding note moves a factor down one point where a specific holding departs from the rated form. Only the rules below apply; nothing is judged case by case. One or two factors moved: market Σ less one. Three or more: less two. Never below Σ1.

6. Evidence rules

Every figure is cited and dated, or absent. No live prices or index levels. Discrepancies between credible sources are shown, not reconciled silently. Inference is tagged as inference; confidence is tagged High, Medium or Low on each figure. Forecasts are recorded as a named house's view on the date made and are never updated. Corrections are new entries, never silent edits.

7. Versioning and records

A change to a definition, a scale, a threshold or the direction of a grade is a new version with a date. Every grade or scale change is entered in the ratings history with its rationale and the version under which it was made. Each brief and each tool output carries the as-of date and the method version it was produced under.
