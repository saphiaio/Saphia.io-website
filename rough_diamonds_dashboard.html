Why Σ4

Rough is not held; it is processed. Price is set by De Beers at each sight and by sealed tender elsewhere, with no public index and no negotiation; parcels are viewed and bid on by manufacturers and dealers who will cut or re-sell within months. A party outside the sight system can buy at tender, which is why this is Σ4 and not Σ5, but it needs a Kimberley Process-registered importer, the capital for a parcel, a cutter and a route to the polished market, and the exit is the same tender room or a Surat factory. Title is clean (KP certificate, invoice) and custody simple; valuation is the problem, since a parcel's worth depends on what it will yield once cut, and that is the buyer's judgement against the seller's price. The practical route to rough exposure is mining equity or a sightholder's business, neither of which is the stone.

IIIMidstream, Credit and the Sight System

Where rough is sold, who may buy it, and why inventory in Surat sets the rough price

Primary rough is sold three ways. De Beers sells about ten times a year at sights to a fixed list of sightholders, now cut from around 70 to 45–50 under the 2026–28 supply agreement, at a box price set by De Beers with no negotiation and payment before release; Alrosa sells by long-term contract and one-off sales; most other producers, and Lucara and Gem Diamonds for large stones, sell by tender, sealed bids on viewed parcels in Antwerp, Gaborone or Dubai, open to any registered buyer with the capital and a Kimberley Process-compliant importer. The tender route is the only way a party outside the sight system acquires rough, and it is a trade, not a holding: the buyer is a manufacturer or a dealer who will cut or re-sell within months.3 4 27 High

What "structural floor" means. The 90–120-day midstream level is the minimum stock cutters & polishers must structurally hold to run their businesses, buying rough is on a 5-week cycle, cutting/polishing takes weeks, and manufacturers must hold mixed assortments to fulfil varied orders. Inventory can balloon above this floor in slow cycles, but rarely dips below it even in supercycles. This locked-in working stock is what generates the bullwhip effect: small downstream demand softening cancels rough orders disproportionately, amplifying volatility upstream.
Sources, inventory days: BCG & De Beers (May 2024); Rapaport podcast Sept 2025 (Narvekar): "retailers typically sit on a year's stock, if not more." Payment terms: Primary rough sales carry no supplier credit, at De Beers sights immediate payment is required with no price negotiation, and tender/auction lots are released only once paid for (GIA Gems & Gemology; De Beers DTC sight history). The credit terms that follow apply downstream (secondary dealer market and midstream→retail), not at the mine: Reserve Bank of India July 2014 norm, clean credit for diamond imports up to 180 days from shipment; Nivoda B2B platform 2025 (Net 30/60/90 standard for midstream→retail); Tiffany & Co. SEC 10-Q filings (30–90 day standard payment terms); industry consensus on memo/consignment (30 days to 6 months). The structural inventory profile is itself the volatility engine: this is why De Beers' rough realised price fell from $152/ct (2024) to $142/ct (2025) despite no proportionate fall in end-consumer demand.2741

◆ Mine Closure Watch · Primary Supply Contraction · Updated 5 Oct 2026
Active Closures, Insolvencies & Care-and-Maintenance, 2025–26
The supply-side tailwind is no longer just structural under-investment, it is an active wave of closures driven by the 2025–26 price trough (lab-grown substitution, US tariffs on India-cut goods, weak China demand) and, since early 2026, a spike in diesel and consumable costs tied to the Middle East conflict. Canada's Northwest Territories, historically ~one-fifth of territorial GDP and ~10% of global rough output, faces going from three producing mines toward potentially zero within the decade; the distress has since spread through southern Africa, Lesotho's largest mine and a flagship Petra operation both pulled back in mid-2026, and in July 2026 De Beers suspended its own flagship, Venetia (~10% of its output), for two years. In parallel, De Beers used its July sale, the first under the 2026–2028 supply agreement, to cut its sightholder base from ~70 to 45–50 and make its deepest-ever official price cuts. ◆ Ownership Transition · Anglo American's Exit from De Beers · Updated 19 Oct 2026
The Sale of De Beers, Status, Structure and Consequences
The single largest structural change facing the industry is not a mine or a price: it is the disposal of the company that built the modern diamond market. Anglo American put its 85% stake in De Beers up for sale in May 2024, as part of the restructuring that followed its defence against BHP's £39bn approach, refocusing the group on copper and iron ore. On 17 July 2026 Botswana's government confirmed Anglo has named a preferred bidder; its due diligence was due to finish at end-September, putting the decision on Botswana's role next.50 The outcome will reset who sets rough prices, how supply is marketed, and how much of the value chain sits with African producer states.51
Preferred bidder named Global Diamond Consortium High Anglo American (LSE: AAL) · 85% stake
Anglo ran a competitive process that narrowed from six groups in 2025 to three shortlisted bidders, then selected the Global Diamond Consortium (GDC), reported by Bloomberg to be led by Gareth Penny, De Beers' chief executive from 2006 to 2010 and now chair of asset manager Ninety One, with Qatari investment-fund backing. Rival groups were led by Nir Livnat (Diacore) and Michael O'Keeffe (non-executive chair of Burgundy Diamond Mines, the Ekati owner). GDC's proposal envisages participation by Angola and Namibia. Botswana's minister Moeti Mohwasa said the priority was an experienced operator with stable long-term backing and a credible turnaround plan. Anglo has declined to confirm details, saying only that a competitive process with multiple bidders continues. Completion is targeted for Q4 2026, subject to conditions including Botswana's approval. Reported terms: about $1bn for the 85% ($750m upfront, $250m deferred), with GDC injecting a further ~$500m after closing.52 Reuters reports two bidders still in contention.50

Near agreement Botswana's right of first refusal High Government of Botswana · 15% holder
This is the variable that decides the deal. Botswana holds 15% of De Beers, half of Debswana, and supplies ~70% of De Beers' rough, and it has a right of first refusal, meaning it can match the winning terms. Mohwasa told parliament the government has "complete freedom" to take one of three paths: join GDC as a partner, pre-empt alone, or pre-empt with a third party. It has hired Lazard and CBH to advise on buying all or part of the company.53 By late September Gaborone had signalled it will raise its 15% holding, even as fiscal constraints on a larger cheque have tightened.50 A right of first refusal inverts the usual auction logic, the preferred bidder does the work of setting price and terms, and the right-holder can then match, which tends to push bidders either to keep offers conservative or to pre-agree a partnership so the deal cannot be topped after the fact. The tension: Botswana said in July 2025 it wanted a controlling stake, but has also stated a policy of diversifying away from diamonds, and analysts have questioned whether increasing exposure to a business in this condition delivers an acceptable short-term return. Namibia's participation is also less settled than it appears: its mines ministry publicly denied in November 2025 that Cabinet had approved any De Beers equity acquisition. Update, 19 Oct 2026: Bloomberg reported on 13 October that Botswana and GDC are close to settling on roughly 25% for the government, with the three producer states together under 50%; private control is a condition for some of GDC's backers. Reuters could not independently verify; Anglo, GDC and the presidency declined to comment. Producer states move up the chain.
