Method

ΣΟΦΙΑ analyses markets that have almost nothing in common. Gold has an exchange, a daily fix and a century of data; saffron has none of these. The method is what makes them comparable.

Every market is read in the same seven-step sequence, to the same evidence rules. That reading produces four structural ratings, and ΣΟΦΙΑ adds one opinion on top: the Σ grade. Each part below is one line; open it for the full thresholds and definitions.

Worked example

Both sit in the same safe and pass through the same jeweller. Read with the same method, one is a phone call to sell and the other waits for a laboratory and an auction.

The sequence

Seven questions, asked of every market in the same order: supply, demand, concentration, price, provenance, liquidity, outlook.

Supply→Demand→Concentration→Liquidity→Scarcity→Provenance→Price formation

A reader who understands the rare-earth separation bottleneck can read the Port benefício, the Habanos monopoly and the De Beers sight as versions of one mechanism. The sequence is what makes those parallels visible.

1SupplyWhat exists, who has it, and what it costs to bring out
2DemandWho buys it, for what, and what they would substitute
3ConcentrationWhere along the chain the power to set price actually sits
4LiquidityWhether a standard unit can be sold, to whom, at a knowable price
5ScarcityWhether supply can respond to price, and over what horizon
6ProvenanceWhether the thing is what the label says, and who verifies it
7Price formationHow those six combine into the price a buyer pays

Two formats. A data-driven page is used where a statistical body publishes a series; an overview page where the market is defined by institutions, history and custom. The format changes the layout, never the sequence, the evidence rules or the standard of proof.

Evidence

What may appear on a page: every figure cited or absent, no live prices, discrepancies shown, inference marked. Source hierarchy, tags, refresh and cadence rules.

Cited or absentEvery number on a page links to a source in the list beneath it, and every source in that list is used somewhere in the text. If a figure matters but no reliable source exists, the page says so and leaves it out rather than guess. Telling you what we don't know is still useful; a number with no source behind it isn't.
No live pricesΣΟΦΙΑ pages are static and would be wrong within days. Historical points appear with their dates and remain as history. Forecasts are recorded as a named house's view at the date made and are never quietly updated, because a forecast that was wrong is more informative than one silently corrected.
Discrepancies shownWhere credible sources conflict, both figures are shown, the one used is stated with the reason, and the confidence is tagged. Silent reconciliation is not permitted. In several markets the disagreement is the finding: the World Platinum Investment Council's own 2025 platinum deficit moved from 966 koz to 692 koz to 1,082 koz across three quarterly reports, and a reader who sees only the final number learns less than one who sees the path.
Inference markedCross-market parallels and ΣΟΦΙΑ's own reasoning are always tagged Hyp. They are never presented as facts about the world.

Refresh and correction

Data as ofEvery page carries the date its figures were compiled. Nothing on a page is presented as current beyond that date.
RefreshA refresh re-verifies figures against current primary sources, updates what has changed with new dates, retains the history, and logs corrections in the relevant source entry.
RatingsSnapshot bands and Σ grades are reviewed at refresh, not between refreshes. A rating that changes carries a note of what changed and why.
CorrectionsErrors are corrected on the page and noted in the source entry, not removed.
Changes noteEach refreshed page ends with a dated note listing what moved, which sources were added and which figures were withdrawn.

Refresh cadence set by how fast the underlying data moves

A page is refreshed when its sources have something new to say, not on a calendar. The cadence follows the slowest input that can change the brief: an exchange price moves daily, an auction result arrives quarterly, a vintage once a year. Every page shows its own "Data as of" date regardless of cadence.

How a market is built

Four criteria-based scales, each Low, Medium or High: supply concentration, liquidity, scarcity, provenance risk. Bands describe structure, are never combined into a score.

Where the power to set price sits, not where the material comes out of the ground. We rate whichever stage of the chain actually holds that power and say plainly which stage it is. Rare earths are rated on separation, not mining; cocoa on two marketing boards, not millions of farms; Port on the benefício and two family groups, not its 19,000 growers.

High
Top three ≥ 75%, or one controller ≥ 50%
Medium
Top three 50–74%
Low
Top three below 50%

Whether a standard unit can be turned into money at a price the seller could have known in advance. Liquidity is rated for the market, not the moment. A whisky cask and a bottle of the same whisky rate differently, and the page says which is rated.

High
Exchange or daily reference price; clears daily
Medium
No exchange, but auction, index or dealer discovery within weeks
Low
Private, irregular or ceremonial sales; no reference price

Whether supply can respond to price, and how quickly. Many of these markets have a scarce apex and an abundant base: wild white truffles against farmed black, Ōma bluefin against ranched. The page rates the apex and says so. Substitution pulls a rating toward Low regardless of what natural supply does.

High
Fixed or falling; cannot expand within 5 years
Medium
Responds with a 3–5 year lag
Low
Responds within 1–2 years, or a substitute exists at scale

The chance that origin, species, grade or lineage is misstated, weighed against the strength of the control that would catch it. A CITES caviar label, a Kimberley Process certificate and a phytosanitary certificate all attest that a document was issued; none attests that the tin, the parcel or the box holds what the document describes. A 2023 genetic study of 149 Lower-Danube caviar samples found 21% illegally wild-caught and 32% deceptive to the consumer, every one of them labelled.

High
Mislabelling above 20% in studies, or no independent check
Medium
Certification exists but is voluntary or cannot detect substitution
Low
Mandatory, independent, forensic control at point of trade

How practical it is to hold

The Σ grade, Σ1 to Σ5: ΣΟΦΙΑ's own opinion of how practical an asset is to hold, weighed across six factors. A house view, provisional, never a view on return.

Σ1InstitutionalIndependent daily valuation, exit within days at low cost, standard custody, established institutional vehicles3 markets
Σ2AccessibleRegular price discovery and an established route to exposure, with one material limit on custody, cost or access7 markets
Σ3SpecialistValued by expert appraisal, exit over months through auction or dealers, material transaction and custody costs19 markets
Σ4RestrictedValuation contested, exit uncertain, heavy authentication, title or transfer burdens20 markets
Σ5No routeThe good cannot be held and no practical route to exposure has been identified2 markets
Six factors:ValuationExitCustodyTitle and transferAccessIntangible premium

What is rated. Where the good itself can be held, the grade covers the physical asset. Where it cannot, because it perishes, is unstorable or has no investable form, the grade covers the most direct route to exposure and names it under the grade, for example "Route: futures" or "Route: listed producer". Where no practical route has been identified, the grade is Σ5.

Six factors scored 1–5; 5 is least burden

ValuationHow independently and how often the asset is pricedScores 5 whenan independent benchmark is set at least daily
ExitHow quickly, and at what cost, a holder can sellScores 5 whena standard unit sells within days at a small spread
CustodyWhat storing and insuring the asset requiresScores 5 whenstandard vaulting or account custody; no decay
Title and transferHow cleanly ownership is proven and passed onScores 5 whenownership is registered or standardised and moves without re-authentication
AccessWhether an investor can buy at all, and through which routeScores 5 whenopen to any investor through regulated vehicles, without sanctions, licences or allocation
Intangible premiumHow much of the price rests on brand, story, provenance or rarity narrativeScores 5 whenthe price is almost entirely the physical good, set by industrial or monetary demand

Why intangible premium is a factor. A premium can disappear without the asset changing: the carbon in a diamond is the same after lab-grown stones arrive, but the natural premium is not. A premium is set by sentiment, so independent valuation is harder and buyers can be scarce when a holder needs to sell. It concentrates fraud risk, because the larger the premium the greater the reward for faking provenance. And it often depends on others to sustain it, through category marketing, brand systems or a ceremonial market. The factor records that the holder carries that risk. It is not a judgement that the premium is wrong or will fall.

Rationale and status. The grade is a judgement across the six factors, not their average, and never a composite with the four scales. Every grade carries a short written rationale on its market page, held to the same evidence rules as any page. Grades, scores and rationales are provisional, reviewed at each refresh; a grade that changes carries a note of what changed and why.
