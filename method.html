How markets are read

Methodology v1.2 · 3 Nov 2026 · The method on one page · Versions ·

Gold has an exchange, a daily price and a century of data. Saffron has none of these. One method, applied the same way to both, is what makes them comparable.

Every market is read with the same seven questions, to the same rules of evidence. The reading produces four scores that describe the market's structure, and one opinion on top: the Σ grade, which says how practical the asset is to own and to sell. Nothing on this page, or anywhere on the site, is a view on return.

Worked example

How does holding pearls differ from holding gold?

Both sit in the same safe and pass through the same jeweller. Read with the same method, one is a phone call to sell and the other waits for a laboratory and an auction.

1 · Seven questions

Asked of every market, in this order. The first two and the last are described in the brief; the middle four are scored.

1SupplyWhat exists, who has it, what it costs to bring out
2DemandWho buys it, for what, and what they would substitute
3Concentration scoredWhere along the chain the power to set price sits
4Liquidity scoredWhether a standard unit can be sold, to whom, at a knowable price
5Scarcity scoredWhether supply can respond to price, and how fast
6Provenance scoredWhether the thing is what the label says, and who checks
7Price formationHow the six combine into the price a buyer pays

2 · Four scores for structure

Each is scored 1 to 5 and shown with its band word: 1–2 Low, 3 Medium, 4–5 High. The scores read in the brief's natural direction, so high concentration is a risk to a buyer and high liquidity a comfort to a seller. They are never added together.

Score5 · High3 · Medium1 · Low
ConcentrationTop three controllers hold 75% or more, or one holds halfTop three hold 50–74%Top three hold under half
LiquidityExchange or daily reference price; clears dailyAuction, index or dealer price within weeksPrivate or irregular sales; no reference price
ScarcitySupply fixed or falling; cannot expand within five yearsSupply responds in three to five yearsSupply responds in one to two years, or a substitute exists at scale
Provenance riskMislabelling above 20% in studies, or no independent checkCertification exists but is voluntary or cannot detect substitutionMandatory, independent, forensic check at point of trade

Concentration is rated at the stage of the chain that sets price, not where the material comes out of the ground: rare earths on separation, cocoa on two marketing boards. Scarcity is rated on the apex tier where one exists. Scores of 2 and 4 are used when the evidence falls clearly between two bands.

3 · The Σ grade

ΣΟΦΙΑ's opinion of how practical the asset is to own and to sell. Σ5 is the most practical, Σ1 the least. It is not a view on value or return: a Σ2 asset can outperform a Σ5 and still be the harder one to own.

GradeMeansAt v1.2
Σ5 · InstitutionalPriced independently every day, sold within days at low cost, standard custody, established vehicles3 markets
Σ4 · AccessibleRegular price discovery and an established route in, with one material limit on custody, cost or access7 markets
Σ3 · SpecialistValued by expert appraisal, sold over months through auction or dealers, material transaction and custody costs19 markets
Σ2 · RestrictedValuation contested, exit uncertain, heavy authentication, title or transfer burdens19 markets
Σ1 · No routeThe good cannot be held and no practical route to exposure has been found2 markets

Six factors behind the grade

Each is scored 1 to 5, with 5 the least burden. The grade is a judgement across the six, not their average: one factor at 1 can hold a grade down on its own, and the brief says which.

FactorWhat it measuresScores 5 when
ValuationHow independently and how often the asset is pricedan independent benchmark is set at least daily
ExitHow quickly, and at what cost, a holder can sella standard unit sells within days at a small spread
CustodyWhat storing and insuring the asset requiresstandard vaulting or account custody; no decay
Title and transferHow cleanly ownership is proven and passed onownership is registered or standardised and moves without re-authentication
AccessWhether an investor can buy at all, and by which routeopen to any investor through regulated vehicles
Intangible premiumHow much of the price rests on brand, story or rarity narrativethe price is almost entirely the physical good

What is graded. The form a holder can actually own, named on the brief. Where the good itself cannot be held, the grade covers the most direct route to exposure and names it, for example "Route: futures". Where no practical route exists, the grade is Σ1.

4 · Two fields beside the grade

Neither is a rating and neither enters the grade. They sit next to Liquidity and say, in plain terms, how long the asset is usually held and what leaving it involves.

FieldWhat it says
Holding horizonHow long the asset is typically held: open-ended for metal in a vault, bounded by the contract for futures, a span of years where maturation or a sale calendar sets it.
Exit depthFour lines for the route named on the brief. Routes: where a holder can sell. Activity: the latest published measure of turnover, dated. Cost: fees, spreads and frictions. Time: from decision to settled cash.

5 · Rules of evidence

RuleIn practice
Cited or absentEvery number links to a source beneath it. If a figure matters but no reliable source exists, the page says so rather than guess.
No live pricesPages are static and dated. Historical points stay as history; forecasts are recorded as a named house's view on the date made and never quietly updated.
Discrepancies shownWhere credible sources conflict, both figures appear and the one used is stated with the reason. Often the disagreement is the finding.
Inference markedΣΟΦΙΑ's own reasoning and cross-market parallels are tagged Hyp, never presented as fact.
Confidence taggedEvery figure carries High primary source, checked; Med secondary or single source; Low vendor or advocacy source, for orientation only; and whether it is Reported, Derived, Estimate or Forecast.
Sources, in orderStatistical agencies, regulators, central banks and company filings first; trade press and auction results with attribution; vendor sizing and industry-funded bodies marked and never headlined.
RefreshA brief is refreshed when its sources have something new to say, not on a calendar: monthly for exchange-priced metals and soft commodities, quarterly for diamonds and gemstones, annually for everything priced at harvest, vintage or auction season. Every page carries its own "Data as of" date.
CorrectionsErrors are corrected on the page and logged, never removed. Grades are reviewed at refresh, and a grade that changes says what changed and why.

6 · Versions

A change to a definition, a scale, a threshold or the direction of a grade is a new version with a date. Grade changes for individual markets are in the ratings history; page corrections in the corrections log.
