Storage, custody and insurance

A scarce asset costs money every day it is held, and in most of the 51 markets that cost is not a fee line but a condition: a humidity, a temperature, a bonded shed, a trainer, a vault. This page reads the Custody and Cost lines together: what has to be done to keep the asset what it is, who does it, what it costs, and where the holding itself is the risk.

IThe carry

In a financial market the cost of holding is called carry and is a number: a management fee, a funding rate, a roll. In the markets covered here it is usually a set of conditions and a bill for meeting them. A case of Bordeaux must stay in a bonded warehouse at a steady temperature or lose its provenance; a box of Cohibas must sit at 65–70% humidity or lose its value; a cask loses about 2% of its contents a year to evaporation and, below 40% abv, its right to be called Scotch; a wheel of Parmigiano must be turned and inspected; a racehorse eats, trains and sees a vet whether or not it ever wins. 20 21 22 23 24

Three things follow. First, the carry sets a floor under the holding horizon: the sector pages for art, cars, watches and whisky bottles all cite five to ten years as the shortest hold that pays, because fees and keeping must be recovered before any gain. Second, custody is often where provenance lives: a bottle out of bond or a bag without its box is the same object with a different price. Third, in a surprising number of markets the keeper is also the market: the bonded warehouse, the Consorzio, the stud, the freeport and the Federation decide what can be sold and in what state.

The register in Section III sorts the 51 markets by what kind of keeping they need. The pattern is that the cheapest custody is where the unit is standard and inert, and the dearest is where the asset is alive, perishable or large enough to need a building of its own.

The paper and vault regimes cost basis points; the shelf regime costs almost nothing but discipline; the conditioned and living regimes cost a percentage of value a year or an operating business. The sector pages' Σ grades run in roughly the same order, not because keeping is graded but because an asset that is expensive to keep is sold by people who must sell, and that shows up in the Liquidity line. VWhere the keeper owns the market

In several markets the place the asset is kept is also the place its price, provenance or title is decided.

The bonded warehouseWine, champagne, port, casks

Bond is where duty is suspended, provenance is recorded and, for casks, title lives: the warehouse keeper's records, not the broker's certificate, say who owns what. When cask firms failed in 2025 the first call was to the warehouse. 22 39

The Consorzio and the bankParmigiano Reggiano

The Consorzio inspects every wheel at twelve months and fire-brands the ones that pass; Credito Emiliano has lent against wheels in its own warehouses since 1953 and sells them through its warehouse subsidiary if the loan is not repaid. The keeper grades, finances and, if needed, liquidates. 23

The FederationMaple syrup

Québec producers must deliver bulk syrup to the Federation in the season it is made; it grades, pays an administered price and decides what ships and what goes to the Global Strategic Maple Syrup Reserve. The keeper is the only buyer. 36

The freeportArt, and increasingly everything

Geneva's freeport, operating since 1888, is estimated to hold about $100bn of goods, some 40% of it art, with wine, diamonds, cars and gold alongside; works can be stored, traded and restored inside without VAT or duty, and the transactions are not reported. The keeper is also a market that publishes no prices. Med 1 2 3

VIThree patterns in the record

Pattern 1Floor

Carry sets the minimum hold

Every sector page that cites a five-to-ten-year horizon does so because fees and keeping must be recovered first. Where carry is a percentage of value a year (cars, horses, farms) the horizon is longer or the asset is a business; where it is basis points (paper, vault) the horizon is a choice.

Watch: bonded storage and insurance rates; training fees; freeport rents

Pattern 2Proof

Custody is where provenance lives

Wine out of bond, a bag out of its box, a cigar box without its seal, a cask outside the warehouse's records, a stone without a fresh report: the object is unchanged and the price is not. The Provenance page found that the Low-risk markets are those where the unit never leaves an accredited chain; this page finds the same markets are the cheapest to keep.

Watch: any market adopting bonded or accredited storage as a condition of sale

Pattern 3Counterparty

The keeper can fail

A bonded warehouse, a trust, a freeport, a trainer and a cask broker are all counterparties. The Exit depth page found that the buyer of last resort is often the party that sold the asset; this page finds that the keeper often is too. The casks page records what happens when that party is insolvent: the asset is intact and the holder's position is not.

Watch: insolvencies among storage and brokerage firms; the uranium trust's discount as the price of custody it will not redeem

VIIIndicators to monitor

  • Insolvencies or title disputes at bonded warehouses and cask brokers, and how the warehouse keeper's records are treated by administrators.
  • Regulation of freeports: the EU's anti-money-laundering rules apply to transactions over €10,000 but do not mandate inventories Med 3; any inventory requirement would change what a freeport is.
  • The Sprott uranium trust's discount to metal, as the clearest live price of custody without redemption 26.
  • Heat and humidity events at ageing warehouses: the 2026 heat waves are the Parmigiano page's live concern 23.
  • Insurance availability for living assets: fertility and mortality cover for stallions, and whether racing and breeding insurers tighten terms.
  • Specialist storage pricing for wine and whisky bottles, which sets the floor under the shelf regime.
