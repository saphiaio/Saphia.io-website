Method

ΣΟΦΙΑ analyses markets that have almost nothing in common. Gold has an exchange, a daily fix and a century of data; saffron has none of these. The method is what makes them comparable.

Who

ΣΟΦΙΑ publishes cited, confidence-rated intelligence on thirty-one scarce-asset markets: diamonds, gold, copper, rare earths, fine wine, Scotch whisky, saffron, caviar, bluefin tuna and the rest. Every number links to a source. Every page carries the date its figures were compiled.

The point is not how many markets we cover. It is that we look at all of them the same way: supply, demand, concentration, liquidity, scarcity, provenance and price formation, in the same order, to the same standard of proof, every time.

No composite score. A composite would require weighting the scales against each other, and no defensible weighting exists across markets as different as copper and truffles. Each rating is published separately and the reader weighs them. Bands describe structure; they are not good or bad.

How practical it is to hold

The Σ grade · Saphia house rating · Provisional

The four scales are criteria-based. The Σ grade is different in kind: it is ΣΟΦΙΑ's own opinion of how practical an asset is to hold in a portfolio. It does not rate expected return and says nothing about where a price is going.

What is rated. Where the good itself can be held, the grade covers the physical asset. Where it cannot, because it perishes, is unstorable or has no investable form, the grade covers the most direct route to exposure and names it under the grade, for example "Route: futures" or "Route: listed producer". Where no practical route has been identified, the grade is Σ5.

Six factors

Each grade is reached by scoring six factors from 1 to 5, where 5 is the least burden on a holder. For futures and listed routes, the scores describe the route, not the physical good.

{{ f.name }} {{ f.asks }} Scores 5 when{{ f.five }}

Why intangible premium is a factor. A premium can disappear without the asset changing: the carbon in a diamond is the same after lab-grown stones arrive, but the natural premium is not. A premium is set by sentiment, so independent valuation is harder and buyers can be scarce when a holder needs to sell. It concentrates fraud risk, because the larger the premium the greater the reward for faking provenance. And it often depends on others to sustain it, through category marketing, brand systems or a ceremonial market. The factor records that the holder carries that risk. It is not a judgement that the premium is wrong or will fall.

Rationale and status

The grade is a judgement across the six factors, not their average, and it is never combined with the four scales into a composite. Every grade carries a short written rationale on its market page: how the asset is valued, how a holder exits, what custody and title involve, and what the binding limit on access is. A rationale is held to the same evidence rules as any page: each factual claim is checked against a dated source, the sources are listed beneath it, and the date of the check is shown.

Status. Σ grades, factor scores and rationales are provisional. They are reviewed at each refresh. A grade that changes carries a note of what changed and why.

What ΣΟΦΙΑ is not

ΣΟΦΙΑ publishes market intelligence and data. It does not publish investment advice, does not accept payment for coverage, and does not carry advertising. Nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything, and no page carries a price target.
