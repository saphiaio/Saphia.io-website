Watches

A CHF 25 billion export industry in which four privately owned brands take half the value, the ultra-high end is the only growing segment, and a secondary market of $17 billion has just finished a three-year correction from its March 2022 peak. This page covers the primary market, concentration, the pre-owned market and its indices, and the structural shift of the trade from retail to resale. No prices or index levels are carried as live values.

The structural facts behind the shares: watches above CHF 50,000 were 37% of export value and 89% of growth on 1.4% of units, while accessible luxury is "under pressure" and the middle tier is compressing.4 5 Rolex is reported to have managed supply downward again in 2025; Patek Philippe rolled back some US prices by up to c.8% after tariff and currency moves.2 Med

Diamond parallel: the sports-steel allocation system, waiting lists at authorised dealers, the Nautilus 5711 trading at multiples of retail, is a sight system run by the brand rather than by a cartel. Rolex's 2023 purchase of Bucherer put the producer inside its own retail and pre-owned channel, the equivalent of a rough producer buying the bourse. Hyp

The correction was a supply story: dealers and individual investors unloaded inventory from mid-2022, and total available inventory for the big three remained far above 2021 levels into 2024.7 8 Morgan Stanley's long-run view was that if the value-retaining brands gradually meet primary demand, most models could trade near or below retail, a view the 2025 stabilisation has tested but not disproved.8 Patek Philippe's Nautilus was reported up 17.2% year on year in Q1 2026, led by the steel 5711/1A.11 Med

Note on data: WatchCharts indices are built from advertised and sold secondary prices, which sit well above what dealers pay to buy. They measure the ask side of the market, not liquidation value.6

Secondary sales are forecast by one industry analyst to surpass primary sales by 2033.13 Low The more defensible statement is that the two markets are now run by the same actors: brands own retailers, retailers own resale platforms, and auction houses trade the same references as the dealers.3 12
