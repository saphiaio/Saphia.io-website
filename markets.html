SupplyStep 1

What exists, who has it, and what it costs to bring out.

Five: supply is fixed or shrinking; nothing more can be made.

LiquidityStep 4

Whether a standard unit can be sold, to whom, at a knowable price.

Five: a standard unit sells within days at a small spread.

ScarcityStep 5

Whether supply can respond to price, and over what horizon.

Five: no price makes more of it; the top tier is counted in hundreds.

ProvenanceStep 6

Whether the thing is what the label says, and who verifies it.

Five: title and authenticity are registered or beyond practical dispute.

Crops

Coffee and cocoa trade on futures against expandable supply; the rest have neither the route nor real scarcity, maple syrup excepted, where one marketing plan sets volume, grade and the only bid.

Rare Foods

Scarcity is real but perishable, so the asset cannot be held; provenance is strongest where a consortium or pedigree register exists, weakest where a CITES paper certifies the document rather than the tin.
