Why Σ3

Third-party grading gives most US and many world coins something no other collectible has: a sealed, numbered, population-tracked unit that trades sight-unseen against published price guides. For those coins exit is days through a dealer or weeks through a major auction. The grade is the limit: a rarity's value is set by one or two bidders, the slab records an opinion not a fact, and the grading companies' own re-grades move prices. Ancients and raw coins carry the usual authentication and, increasingly, export-law risk.

Coins are where the collectible meets the commodity. A common-date gold sovereign or double eagle trades at a small premium to its metal; a 1933 double eagle, of which one may legally be owned, made $18.9 million. Between them sits a market organised around two grading companies, two auction houses and a calendar of shows, with better price data than any other field on this site. This page covers size and cycle, the record book, how grading works and what it does not do, the trade's structure, and what to watch. Bullion coins as metal are covered under Gold and Silver. ISize and cycle

Measured better than any collectible, and tied to the gold price.

Heritage and Stack's Bowers together sell well over $1 bn of coins a year at auction, and Heritage publishes totals by category; the dealer market through the shows and the two grading companies' population reports give the trade a transparency no other collecting field has. The cycle follows two things: the gold and silver price, which sets the floor under every coin of precious metal and drives new money into the field when it rises, as in 2024–26; and the appearance of named cabinets, Eliasberg, Pogue, Norweb, Bruun, Stack, which produce the records.16 High for auction totals; Med for the whole market.

The 1933 double eagle is a legal title, not a coin market. Of fourteen known, thirteen are government property and any new one is seized; the $18.9 m price is what the one lawful example brought, and there is no second. The 1804 dollar, with fifteen known in three classes, is the better guide to what the top of the market will pay repeatedly: $7.68 m (2021), $6 m (2025). High35

IIIGrading: the slab and the sticker

The institution that made coins tradeable, and its limits.

Since 1986 PCGS and since 1987 NGC have graded coins on the 70-point Sheldon scale, sealed them in tamper-evident holders with a serial number, and published population reports counting how many of each coin they have graded at each level. CAC, founded 2007, adds a sticker to coins it judges solid for the grade and since 2023 grades in its own holders. The system makes a certified common coin close to fungible: dealers buy and sell by grade from price guides without seeing the coin.3 High

Parallel to diamonds: the grading report is the GIA certificate of coins, and it has done the same thing, turned an expert's opinion into a traded unit and shifted value from the object to the paper. The difference is that a diamond's grade does not change on resubmission; a coin's can. Hyp
