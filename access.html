Fractional and fund access

Most scarce assets cannot be bought in a brokerage account. Between the holder and the asset sits a wrapper: a fund, a trust, a listed producer, a syndicate, a platform share or a token. This page reads the wrappers recorded on the market pages together: what each actually owns, what it costs, how it exits, and which of them have failed.

IWhat a wrapper changes

A wrapper does three things for a holder. It divides an asset too large or too lumpy to own alone: a stallion into forty shares, a painting into Reg A securities, a trust's uranium into units. It delegates custody, insurance and sale to a manager who is paid for it. And it substitutes a market in the wrapper for a market in the asset: the holder sells units, not metal; shares, not a horse.

The third is where the record on the sector pages is decisive. Where the asset has a benchmark price and a standard unit, the market in the wrapper tracks the market in the asset and the wrapper adds liquidity: a gold ETF trades at a spread near 0.01% to a price set twice a day. 20 Where it does not, the wrapper acquires a price of its own that can sit far from the asset: the uranium trust at a 13% discount to its metal, a Comvita share that moved between 43c and 82c in a year while the honey sat in a warehouse, a fractional sports card "50–70% below purchase price" with "zero secondary market liquidity". 21 22 2

The Tokenisation page found that a token cannot create a price, a buyer or an exit. The same is true of every wrapper on this page. What a wrapper can do is pool, delegate and list; what it cannot do is make a non-standard asset standard. The register in Section III sorts the markets by which wrappers exist and what they own.

IVThe failures

Three wrapper families built for non-standard assets, and what happened to them. The pattern is the same each time: the wrapper had a price only while the platform made one.

Fractional collectibles2018–2026

Dozens of thin order books is not liquidity

Otis was acquired by Public.com in March 2022 and its single-asset series wound down, one selling two NFTs for $1,039 against $6,400 raised. Collectable has not run an offering since 2022 and is in Delaware Chancery litigation with secondary trading halted. Rally, the last operating platform, carried a going-concern qualification in its parent's 2025 audit, reported a 1.20x median exit across 111 closed positions, and in July 2026 began winding down its fractional model to launch a listed collectibles fund. Most sports-card series trade 50–70% below purchase with no secondary liquidity Low 5

Cask investment2019–2025

A certificate is a claim against the seller

Firms selling casks and cask portfolios to retail raised about £80m before Cask 88, Braeburn Whisky and Whisky Merchants Trading entered insolvency in April–May 2025. The holder's position was a certificate from the firm; ownership was whatever the bonded warehouse's records said. The sector page rates provenance risk Very high for title, duplication and existence. 27 30

Watch: administrators' treatment of certificate holders against warehouse records

Tokenised collectibles2015–2026

No price to redeem at

Everledger raised $51.7m for diamond provenance and entered administration in 2023; on-chain tokenised assets show "minimal secondary trading" outside gold; gemstone-token redemption spreads of 8–15% are cited by one law firm. The Tokenisation page records that the markets without a transaction-based price have no working token, and the ones with one (gold) did not need it for liquidity. 29

Watch: VARA approvals and any secondary volume in issued gemstone tokens

Derived observation

Masterworks is the exception that proves the rule and the sector page states why: its shares are securities in a single work, and its exit is still the sale of the whole painting. It has survived because it is a fund manager that sells paintings, not a market in fractions. Rally's 2026 pivot to a listed fund is the same conclusion reached the hard way. Hyp 28 3

VIThree patterns in the record

Pattern 1Benchmark

A wrapper is as liquid as the price beneath it

Gold, silver and platinum funds trade at a hair from the metal because the metal has a price. The uranium trust holds real metal and still trades 13% away, because the spot price is a consultants' estimate from a thin market. Every wrapper on this page inherits the liquidity of its underlying and adds nothing to it; the price regime decides the wrapper's fate before it launches.

Watch: the uranium discount; any transaction-based index in diamonds or wine that would make a fund trackable

Pattern 2Dilution

The listed producer is a company, not the asset

In a dozen markets the only wrapper is a company that also does other things: Ito En is a beverage group, Symrise a flavour house, Maruha Nichiro a seafood conglomerate, Anglo American a diversified miner. The holder gets management, debt, currency and every other product line alongside the exposure sought. Comvita shows the pure-play alternative: a stock that moved 90% in a year on corporate events while the honey price did not.

Watch: Deoleo's ownership, Comvita's recapitalisation, De Beers' sale

Pattern 3Platform

Where the platform makes the market, the platform is the risk

Fractional series, cask certificates and gemstone tokens all had a price only on the platform that issued them, and the Exit depth page found the same party is usually the buyer of last resort. When Otis, Collectable and the cask firms stopped, the assets were intact and the holders' positions were not. The Custody page reaches the same conclusion about keepers; this page reaches it about wrappers.

Watch: going-concern language in platform audits; Rally's fund NAV at conversion

VIIIndicators to monitor

  • The terms on which Rally rolls legacy series into its listed fund, and whether the fund trades at a discount to its holdings 3.
  • Masterworks' exit count and realised returns against its own reported figures, which are marked by the company as pending confirmation 28.
  • Administrators' reports on the 2025 cask-firm insolvencies: recoveries to certificate holders 27.
  • Growth of tokenised gold relative to gold ETFs, the test of whether a new wrapper adds liquidity where a benchmark exists 29.
  • Any wine or coin fund publishing audited realised returns rather than index performance.
  • Whether stallion shares or racing syndicates acquire a secondary venue; the Flightline share at Keeneland in 2022 was a public auction of a syndicate interest, and rare 26.
