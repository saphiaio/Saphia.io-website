Why Σ3

A quota Birkin or Kelly in a liquid configuration has a readier market than almost any collectible: dealers and platforms quote buy prices, Sotheby's runs continuous online sales, and a store-fresh bag clears in weeks. The grade stops at Σ3 because everything that makes the market work is controlled by one company, Hermès, which sets retail prices, decides who may buy, and has no obligation to keep the premium alive; because the premium itself is narrowing; and because the bags are the most counterfeited luxury object on earth.

The only consumer product that routinely resells above its retail price, and the clearest example on this site of scarcity created by allocation rather than by nature. Hermès makes as many Birkins as it chooses, sells them only to clients it chooses, and the difference between the boutique price and the price a stranger will pay is the market. This page covers how the allocation works, the size and current state of the resale market, the record book, authentication and condition, and what to watch. No prices are carried as live values.

IOne house, one allocation system

Hermès does not sell a Birkin to a walk-in customer; that is the whole market.

Hermès produces Birkins and Kellys in its own French workshops, one artisan per bag, and offers them to clients through the boutique at the sales associate's discretion. In practice a "quota bag" (one or two a year per client) follows a history of purchases in other categories, the "pre-spend"; the practice is the subject of a 2024 US class action alleging tying. The result is a retail price well below what the market will pay, and a resale premium that is the difference. Hermès raises retail prices annually, which lifts the resale floor beneath every bag already sold.34 High

IISize and the 2025–26 cycle

The curated top and the average are telling different stories.

Sotheby's, the venue that sold the original Birkin, reports Birkin and Kelly sales up 44% in 2025 and 55% since 2023, with average selling prices up around 35% in a year and nearly $160 m sold since 2021. Bernstein's measure across all auction Birkins shows the average premium to retail falling from 2.2× in 2022 to about 1.4× in late 2025. Both are right: the auction house measures store-fresh quota bags in liquid configurations; the analyst measures everything, in a secondary market with more supply than at any time as 2021–22 buyers sell and platforms multiply. Saclàb's mid-2026 average of €16,290 for regular-leather bags sits close to current retail.23 High

Parallel to watches and signed jewellery: a serial system, a house archive and a secondary market organised by the houses' own scarcity.
