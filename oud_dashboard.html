IWhat it is

A pathology, not a species.

Agarwood is resin-saturated heartwood produced by trees of the genus Aquilaria (and related Gyrinops and Gonystylus) in response to wounding and fungal infection. Healthy trees contain none. In the wild, a small fraction of mature trees form resin; the rest are felled to find out, since most harvesting, wild or cultivated, destroys the whole tree.3 Trees need up to a decade to reach maturity.3 High

FormUseWhere the value sits
Chips and wood (bakhoor)Burned as incense; Gulf and East Asian ceremonial useGraded by resin density, origin and age; the ultra-rare kynam/kyara grade sits at the top
Oil (dahn al-oud)Distilled; worn neat as attar or dosed into fine fragranceDistillation yield is low; blends with cultivated material are common

Three species, A. malaccensis (Critically Endangered, India/Malaysia/Indonesia), A. crassna (Critically Endangered, Indochina) and A. filaria (Vulnerable, eastern Indonesia and Papua), account for more than 90% of recorded chip and powder trade.2 High

IISupply: wild versus cultivated

Plantations have solved the volume problem for one species and not for the others.

Since about 2017 the trade has shifted toward artificially inoculated plantation wood as large estates in Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Cambodia and north-east India matured.2 5 Cultivated wood is younger, lower in resin density and cheaper; it supplies the oil and incense-stick segments but does not substitute for old wild chips at the top of the market. Med

Population data are stale. Indonesia's 2009 non-detriment finding used 2001–03 surveys assuming 10 trees per hectare; most published estimates put normal density below 1–2 trees per hectare, implying the country's export quota rested on a population overstated by an order of magnitude.3 A 2025 submission to the CITES Standing Committee found many exporters still using NDFs more than a decade old.7 Med

Diamond parallel: the Gulf is to oud what Antwerp was to rough, the place where wild material from a dozen origins is aggregated, graded by eye and sold on trust. There is no equivalent of a GIA report. A credible grading and provenance standard for chips (resin density, species DNA, cultivated/wild, age) is an open commercial opportunity, and the DNA-database work done for A. malaccensis in Malaysia shows the forensic tools already exist.12 Hyp

For a UAE importer the practical exposure is the same as with gold doré or rough diamonds: origin is asserted at the point of export, not verified at the point of import. A CITES permit proves that a permit was issued; it does not prove that the wood in the crate is the wood on the permit. For an MLRO the relevant questions are species declaration, source code (W wild / A artificially propagated), and whether the exporter's NDF is current. Med
